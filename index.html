Syllabus & reference texts

Linear Statistical Models 0/12

Instructor's handout — Debashis Paul, Semester 1, 2026–27

  1. Gauss–Markov framework for linear statistical models.
  2. Least squares estimation; normal equations; algebraic properties of residuals; degrees of freedom.
  3. Estimability of linear functions; best linear unbiased estimator (BLUE); Gauss–Markov Theorem.
  4. Considerations for linear regression: categorical predictors; variable influence; outliers.
  5. Analysis of variance approach to linear models; Fisher–Cochran Theorem.
  6. Testing of linear hypotheses; equivalence of classical test procedures for linear hypotheses.
  7. Confidence sets for linear functionals.
  8. Resampling-based inference techniques.
  9. One-way and two-way classified data; ANOVA and ANCOVA.
  10. Multiple comparisons with applications to regression and ANOVA.
  11. Introduction to random and mixed effects models.
  12. Regularization and model selection schemes for linear regression.
Reference texts
  • R. Christensen — Plane Answers to Complex Questions: The Theory of Linear Models (Springer)
  • J. Faraway — Linear Models with R (Taylor & Francis)
  • J. Jiang — Linear and Generalized Linear Mixed Models and Their Applications (Springer)
  • C. R. Rao and H. Toutenburg — Linear Models: Least Squares and Alternatives (Springer)
  • S. R. Searle — Linear Models (Wiley)
  • D. Sengupta and S. R. Jammalamadaka — Linear Models: An Integrated Approach (World Scientific)
Parametric Inference 0/5

B.Stat (2016) brochure — official syllabus

  1. Basic inference problems. Sufficiency, factorization theorem, minimal sufficiency. Completeness, Lehmann–Scheffé Theorem. Ancillarity, Basu's Theorem. Exponential families of distributions, canonical parameters and canonical sufficient statistics.
  2. Point estimation: criteria for goodness — mean square error, unbiasedness, relative efficiency, Cramér–Rao inequality, Bhattacharya bounds, UMVUE, Rao–Blackwell theorem. Consistency.
  3. Bayesian techniques: priors, posteriors, Bayes' estimators and Bayesian credible regions.
  4. Tests of hypotheses: statistical hypothesis, simple and composite hypotheses, critical regions. Neyman–Pearson Lemma and MP test; randomization; UMP, UMPU and LMP tests; illustrations. Monotone likelihood ratio family of distributions.
  5. Likelihood ratio tests. Test of multiple hypotheses, union–intersection principle.
Reference texts
  • P. J. Bickel and K. A. Doksum — Mathematical Statistics
  • G. Casella and R. L. Berger — Statistical Inference
  • C. R. Rao — Linear Statistical Inference and its Applications
  • E. L. Lehmann — Theory of Point Estimation
  • E. L. Lehmann — Testing Statistical Hypotheses
Sample Surveys 0/6

B.Stat (2016) brochure — official syllabus

  1. Concepts of population, sample, survey and census. Sampling designs and schemes.
  2. Properties of good estimators under different approaches: design, predictive, super-population-modelling and model-assisted. Sampling strategies.
  3. Drawing simple random samples (SRS) with and without replacement using random numbers; estimation; sample size determination.
  4. Narain–Horvitz–Thompson estimator. Sen–Yates–Grundy estimator. Stratified sampling, cluster sampling, multi-stage sampling. PPS sampling — WR and WOR. Systematic sampling — equal and unequal probabilities, linear and circular, unbiased variance estimation.
  5. Ratio and regression estimation for equal and unequal probability sampling; Hartley–Ross estimator. Interpenetrating Network of Subsampling (IPNS) and half-sampling.
  6. Double sampling — non-response and 'not-at-homes'. Sampling on successive occasions. Acquaintance with National Sample Surveys and other large-scale surveys; controlling non-sampling errors.
Reference texts
  • W. G. Cochran — Sampling Techniques
  • M. N. Murthy — Sampling Theory and Methods
  • A. Chaudhuri — Essentials of Survey Sampling
  • A. S. Hedayat and B. K. Sinha — Design and Inference in Finite Population Sampling
  • C. M. Cassel, C. E. Särndal and J. H. Wretman — Foundations of Inference in Survey Sampling
EOS&D · Economic Statistics 0/4

B.Stat (2016) brochure — official syllabus

  1. Index numbers: construction of index numbers, properties, well-known index number formulae, problems of construction, chain indices, cost of living indices, splicing of index numbers, different types of index numbers used in India.
  2. Analysis of income and allied size distributions: Pareto and log-normal distributions — genesis, specification and estimation. Lorenz curve, Gini coefficient.
  3. Demand analysis: classification of commodities, Engel curve analysis using cross-section and time series data, Engel curves incorporating household characteristics, demand projection, specific concentration curves.
  4. Production analysis: profit maximization, cost minimization, returns to scale, Cobb–Douglas and ACMS production functions.
Reference texts
  • P. H. Karmel and M. Polasek — Applied Statistics for Economists
  • R. G. D. Allen — Price Index Numbers
  • N. Kakwani — Income Inequality and Poverty
  • L. R. Klein — An Introduction to Econometrics
  • J. S. Cramer — Empirical Econometrics
  • M. D. Intrilligator — Econometric Models, Techniques and Applications
EOS&D · Official Statistics 0/3

B.Stat (2016) brochure — official syllabus

  1. Indian Statistical System: official organisations collecting, compiling and publishing national and state level data — CSO, NSSO, RBI, Planning Commission, State Statistical Bureaus, Labour Bureau, Population Census; role of Centre and State.
  2. Selected topics on Indian statistics: agriculture and allied areas including meteorology and environment; industry, trade and finance including money supply and banking; national accounts and infrastructure; population, health, education, prices, level of living, labour and employment.
  3. International Statistical System: comparison of major macro variables — National Income / GDP. Purchasing power parity; indicators relating to energy, environment, gender, industry, national accounts, social statistics and trade.
Reference texts
  • M. R. Saluja — Indian Official Statistical Systems
  • CSO (MoSPI) — Statistical System in India
  • United Nations publications
  • RBI — Handbook of Statistics for the Indian Economy (various years)
  • Economic Survey, Govt. of India, Ministry of Finance (various years)
EOS&D · Demography 0/5

B.Stat (2016) brochure — official syllabus

  1. Sources of demographic data — census, registration of vital events. Rates and ratios. Measures of mortality.
  2. Life table — construction and applications.
  3. Stable and stationary population. Measures of fertility and reproduction. Standardization of vital rates.
  4. Population growth curves, population estimates and projections.
  5. Measures of migration. Use of demographic data for policy formulation.
Reference texts
  • R. Ramkumar — Technical Demography
  • K. Srinivasan — Demographic Techniques and Applications
  • B. D. Mishra — An Introduction to the Study of Population
  • H. S. Shryock — The Methods and Materials in Demography
Design and Analysis of Algorithms 0/7

B.Stat (2016) brochure — official syllabus

  1. Introduction and basic concepts: complexity measures and asymptotic notation, notions of worst-case and average-case complexity, use of recurrences in algorithms. Searching algorithms: binary search, balanced binary search tree, hashing.
  2. Selection and sorting: finding maximum and minimum, k-th largest elements. Different sorting algorithms — quicksort, mergesort, heapsort. Lower bound for sorting. Other sorting algorithms — radix sort, bucket sort.
  3. Graph algorithms: basic definitions, connectivity and traversals (Breadth First Search and Depth First Search), directed acyclic graphs and topological ordering.
  4. Computational geometry: convex hull, diameter of a point set.
  5. Greedy algorithms: shortest paths in a graph, minimum spanning trees, clustering.
  6. Divide and conquer: closest pair of points, integer multiplication, matrix multiplication, Fast Fourier Transform.
  7. Dynamic programming: subset sum, knapsack, all pair shortest paths in a graph.
Reference texts

  • J. Kleinberg and E. Tardos — Algorithm Design
  • S. Dasgupta, C. Papadimitriou, U. Vazirani — Algorithms
  • A. V. Aho, J. E. Hopcroft and J. D. Ullman — The Design and Analysis of Computer Algorithms
  • E. Horowitz, S. Sahni and S. Rajasekaran — Computer Algorithms
  • D. E. Knuth — The Art of Computer Programming: Fundamental Algorithms
  • A. V. Aho, J. E. Hopcroft and J. D. Ullman — Data Structures and Algorithms
